id: SRC-EMPIRICAL-029
authors: Nancy Rogers, Clifton Callender
year: 2006
title: "Judgments of distance between trichords"
venue: Proceedings of the 9th International Conference on Music Perception & Cognition
source_type: conference_paper
doi:
url: https://myweb.fsu.edu/ccallender/icmpc-proceedings.pdf
verification_status: VERIFIED
exact_construct: "Perceived musical distance between successive trichords."
task_repertoire: "Synthetic trichords presented in Shepard tones; register and spacing effects were reduced."
task: "Listeners heard pairs of trichords in immediate succession and rated perceived distance."
dependent_measure: "Distance rating."
manipulated_variables: "Number of moving voices, displacement size, common-tone retention, direction, relationship of motion and tuning environment."
what_supports: "Total voice-leading motion correlated with greater perceived distance and more common tones reduced perceived distance in the inspected paradigms; displacement did not combine as a simple linear taxicab metric."
what_does_not_support: "Does not validate perceived distance as continuity, does not establish minimum motion as universal optimum, and does not generalize from synthetic trichords to popular-song realization."
important_limitations:
  - "Distance task, not continuity or connectedness task."
  - "Trichords and Shepard tones; register and spacing were controlled away."
  - "Not a comparison of full voicings in realistic texture."
