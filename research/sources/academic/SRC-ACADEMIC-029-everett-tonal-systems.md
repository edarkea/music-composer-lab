exact_construct: "Six tonal systems for rock (Table 1): Types 1a/1b major-minor; Type 2 modal; Type 3a mixture/quasi-modal; Type 3b progressive modulation; Type 4 blues (major-mode structure); Type 5 triad-doubled minor-pentatonic (non-functional); Type 6 chromatic/asserted."
task_repertoire: "Close readings across rock history plus a 6000+ song 1950s–60s background study (§3, NEH fellowship work). Analyst's framework; NOT an experiment."
what_supports: "No single tonal behavior common to all rock (§5). Modal systems with directed melody but no harmonic pull to tonic ('Sounds of Silence' Aeolian, no harmonic function; 'Tomorrow Never Knows' Mixolydian I–bVII–I over unmoving tonic pedal, §10). Pentatonic music tonally ambiguous; centers dependent on assertion rather than syntax (§6). Descending-fourth chromatic sequences establish neither scale nor tonal center (§11). Type 5 non-functional harmony: tonic status suggested by metric assertion and melodic goal-directedness (§20). Type 6: tonic by assertion rather than syntax. >98% of known pop-rock works classifiable as tonal in the broad sense (§5)."
what_does_not_support: "No listener measurements; no cue-weight quantification; Schenkerian comparisons are analytical, not perceptual evidence."
important_limitations:
