# Research Roadmap — General Composition Knowledge Acquisition

## Purpose

This roadmap defines the research plan for acquiring **general composition knowledge** that will later be specialized by genre. It is organized around **compositional problems and decisions**, not theory chapters.

The architecture:

```
GENERAL COMPOSITION KNOWLEDGE (manual/)
    → GENRE SPECIALIZATION (genres/)
    → CONCRETE COMPOSITION DECISIONS
    → SongPlan
    → music-engine
```

---

## 0. Scope of "General"

**Initial scope:** General composition knowledge within **Western tonal/modal popular music and closely related traditions** (common-practice tonality, pop, rock, jazz, electronic/dance, and related traditions where transferability can be demonstrated).

**Important:** `general` does NOT mean `universal to all human music`.

Research must explicitly mark when:
- a theory belongs to a particular tradition;
- an empirical result uses listeners enculturated in a specific tradition;
- a heuristic likely does not transfer outside this scope.

Do not attempt to build a cross-cultural universal theory at this stage.

If you find language in other project documents that contradicts this distinction, do NOT rewrite it automatically in this task: report it.

## 1. Composition Domains and Research Topics

### 1.1 Melody

| Research Topic | Compositional Questions |
|---|---|
| **Melodic Identity** | What makes a melodic idea recognizable? What features must persist across variations for perceptual kinship? |
| **Motif / Cell** | What is the minimal unit of melodic identity? How do composers construct and deploy cells? |
| **Phrase** | What distinguishes a phrase from a mere sequence of notes? How do phrase functions (antecedent/consequent, opening/closing) map to compositional decisions? |
| **Contour** | How does contour shape perception of direction, tension, and closure? What contour types serve which functions? |
| **Interval Structure** | How do step vs. leap distributions affect continuity, emphasis, and memorability? When are leaps compensatory vs. structural? |
| **Rhythm as Part of Melody** | How does rhythmic profile contribute to identity independently of pitch? What rhythmic features create hook-like recognizability? |
| **Register / Range** | How do register choices affect perceived energy, intimacy, and structural function? How does range expansion/contraction create contrast? |
| **Repetition** | How much repetition establishes identity before variation is needed? What repetition patterns (AAA, AABA, ABAC) serve which functions? |
| **Variation** | What dimensions can vary while preserving identity? What is the perceptual threshold where variation becomes a new idea? |
| **Development** | How do fragmentation, extension, reduction, recombination, and transformation create large-scale coherence? |
| **Direction** | How do composers create a sense of "going somewhere" at phrase and section levels? |
| **Climax** | What combination of register, duration, density, harmony, and timing creates a perceived climax? How is it prepared? |
| **Closure** | What melodic features signal continuation vs. partial closure vs. strong arrival? How do they interact with harmony? |
| **Continuity / Contrast** | How do composers balance sameness and difference within and between phrases? |

### 1.6 Tension / Release / Expectation

> **Principle:** Do NOT treat tension as a single scalar. Map contributors from each domain.

| Domain | Potential Tension Contributors |
|---|---|
| **Melody** | High register, wide leaps, unresolved tendency tones, dense contour, delayed resolution |
| **Harmony** | Dissonance, non-functional chords, delayed cadence, chromaticism, modal ambiguity |
| **Rhythm** | Syncopation, metric displacement, density increase, subdivision acceleration, silence before downbeat |
| **Register** | Upward registral shift, extreme registers, registral compression/expansion |
| **Density** | Note-onset density, layer count, textural thickening |
| **Dissonance** | Interval/triad quality, non-chord tones, harmonic complexity |
| **Timing** | Phrase extension, elision, ritardando/accelerando, fermata |
| **Expectation** | Schema violation, deferred resolution, pattern break |
| **Form** | Sectional boundary delay, unexpected return, missing expected section |

**Research Questions:**
- How do these contributors combine? Additively? Interactively?
- Which are necessary vs. sufficient for perceived tension?
- How does genre modulate the mapping from acoustic features to perceived tension?
- What creates the *feeling* of release vs. merely structural resolution?

**Framework policy:** Do NOT adopt ITPRA, Meyer, Farbood, Lerdahl, or any other single framework as the project's "official" tension model. They may be studied as partial models explaining different mechanisms. Do not create a single `tension: 0.0–1.0` variable without foundation.

### 1.7 Hooks / Prominence / Memorability

> Keep these constructs **separate**. Do not merge them unless sufficient evidence supports linking them.

Assume neither:
- hook = prominence
- hook = memorability
- prominence = memorability

Each relationship must be investigated separately.

| Construct | Research Questions |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | What makes a musical idea function as a "hook" (immediate recognizability, repeatability, distinctiveness)? |
| **Prominence** | What makes a moment stand out in real-time listening (accent, register, timbre, harmony, surprise)? |
| **Memorability** | What features correlate with post-exposure recall? How do repetition, simplicity, distinctiveness, and emotional salience interact? |

## 2. Domain Dependencies

Dependencies are **partial and contextual**, not a strict hierarchy. The evidence does not justify a single total order.

```
Event Parameters (pitch, duration, velocity, timbre, onset)
    ↓
Derived Descriptors (interval, contour, density, syncopation, harmonic rhythm)
    ↓
Motif / Phrase Relations (identity, similarity, variation, contrast)
    ↓
Repetition / Variation Strategies (literal, varied, fragmented, extended)
    ↓
Development Techniques (fragmentation, recombination, transformation, accumulation)
    ↓
Formal Structure (phrase grouping, sectional function, large-scale trajectory)
    ↓
Genre Realization (which general strategies are prioritized, avoided, combined)
```

**Key caveats:**
- Harmony can influence melody *and* melody can imply harmony (bidirectional)
- Rhythm operates at surface and structural levels simultaneously
- Texture/arrangement decisions can precede or follow melodic/harmonic ones
- Genre conventions can override general principles at any level

## 3. Research Workflow (Per Question)

For each research question, follow this repeatable workflow:

1. **Define Compositional Question** — What decision does the composer face?
2. **Define Terminology** — Precisely define key terms; note theoretical context.
3. **Search Existing Literature/Theory** — Academic music theory, composition pedagogy, music psychology, ethnomusicology.
4. **Identify Empirical Findings** — Perceptual/cognitive studies with experimental evidence.
5. **Identify Established Theoretical Descriptions** — What do established theories (Schenker, Gjerdingen, Caplin, Lerdahl/Jackendoff, Temperley, Huron, etc.) say?
6. **Inspect Serious Pedagogical/Compositional Heuristics** — What do practicing composers/teachers recommend? (e.g., Schoenberg, Rimsky-Korsakov, modern songwriting guides).
7. **Inspect Repertoire/Genre Evidence** — When appropriate, analyze corpus or specific works.
8. **Extract Candidate Claims** — Formulate as: *compositional problem → decision variable → possible effect → context → evidence class*.
9. **Classify Evidence** — Use project evidence classes (THEORY, GENRE OBSERVATION, COMPOSITION HEURISTIC, HYPOTHESIS, EXPERIMENTAL RESULT, ARTISTIC PREFERENCE).
10. **Separate STRUCTURE → PERCEPTION → COMPOSITION** — Keep these layers explicit.
11. **Identify Contradictions and Boundary Conditions** — Where does the claim fail? What genres/contexts contradict it?
12. **Identify Actionable Compositional Implications** — What should a composer *do* with this?
13. **Identify Remaining Uncertainty** — What is still unknown?
14. **Determine Whether an Experiment Is Actually Necessary** — Only if uncertainty is high and falsifiable.
15. **After Review, Promote to `manual/` / `rules/`** — Only after human + Music/Methodology Director review.

## 4. Evidence Classes

Use the project's six classes. **Classify CLAIMS, not entire topics.**

| Class | Definition |
|---|---|
| **THEORY** | Established concept within a specific theoretical tradition. Context must be indicated. |
| **GENRE OBSERVATION** | Pattern observed in one/more genres. Not universal. |
| **COMPOSITION HEURISTIC** | Practical recommendation useful in certain contexts. Not a law. |
| **HYPOTHESIS** | Proposition insufficiently tested. Candidate for research/experiment. |
| **EXPERIMENTAL RESULT** | Result from project's own experiments. Must preserve conditions, variables, results, interpretation, limitations. |
| **ARTISTIC PREFERENCE** | Project Owner's aesthetic decision. Valid artistically, not general knowledge. |

**Critical rule:** When external empirical research supports a perceptual claim, preserve that distinction clearly rather than silently converting it into a compositional rule.

---

## 4b. Evidence Class vs. Support Basis (Orthogonal Dimension)

In addition to `evidence_class`, every claim carries a `support_basis` field indicating **where the support for the claim comes from**. These are orthogonal dimensions — a single claim can have multiple support bases.

### Support Basis Categories

- **EXTERNAL_EMPIRICAL** — Peer-reviewed perceptual/cognitive experiments, corpus studies with statistical analysis
- **THEORETICAL_SCHOLARSHIP** — Established music theory, analysis frameworks (Schenker, Gjerdingen, Caplin, Lerdahl/Jackendoff, Temperley, etc.)
- **COMPOSITION_PEDAGOGY** — Conservatory textbooks, composer treatises, serious songwriting/composition guides
- **CORPUS_ANALYSIS** — Systematic computational or manual analysis of repertoires with clear methodology
- **REPERTOIRE_ANALYSIS** — Close analytical study of specific works/recordings (not necessarily systematic corpus)
- **PROJECT_EXPERIMENT** — This project's own controlled experiments (EXP-001, etc.)
- **PRACTITIONER_OBSERVATION** — Documented insights from working composers/producers, labeled as such

### Example

```yaml
evidence_class: COMPOSITION HEURISTIC
support_basis:
  - THEORETICAL_SCHOLARSHIP
  - EXTERNAL_EMPIRICAL
```

This means the claim is a compositional recommendation (heuristic), and its support comes from both theoretical scholarship and external empirical research.

**Do not confuse** `evidence_class` (what kind of knowledge claim it is) with `support_basis` (what evidence supports it).

## 6. Source Quality Guidelines

Prioritize:

1. **Primary academic literature** — peer-reviewed music theory, music psychology, cognitive science, ethnomusicology.
2. **Established music-theory/composition scholarship** — Caplin, Gjerdingen, Hepokoski/Darcy, Lerdahl/Jackendoff, Schenker, Schoenberg, Riepel, Koch, Temperley, Huron, Margulis, etc.
3. **Reputable academic books** — Oxford Handbooks, Cambridge Companions, monographs from university presses.
4. **Serious pedagogical/compositional literature** — Textbooks used in conservatory curricula, composer treatises.
5. **Systematic repertoire analysis** — Corpus studies with clear methodology (e.g., Temperley, de Clercq, London Billboard corpus).
6. **Genre observation** — Documented patterns with specific repertoire references.

**Do NOT build foundational claims from:**
- SEO blogs
- Generic AI-generated theory pages
- Unsupported social-media opinions
- Unverified "rules" from non-specialist sources

Community/practitioner insight can be retained when useful but **must be labeled** as `GENRE OBSERVATION` or `COMPOSITION HEURISTIC` with source attribution.

## 7. Critical Epistemic Rule

> **Knowing a structural/theoretical fact does NOT automatically establish its perceptual or compositional effect.**

| Layer | Example | Does Not Imply |
|---|---|---|
| **STRUCTURE** | A dominant chord contains scale degrees 5, 7, 2, 4 | — |
| **PERCEPTION** | Listeners necessarily experience specific tension | STRUCTURE alone |
| **COMPOSITION** | A composer should use V7 at phrase endings | PERCEPTION alone |

**Keep these layers explicit in every candidate claim.** The `structure_perception_composition` field in the candidate format enforces this.

## 9. Initial Research Priority — Melody Foundations

After creating this roadmap, the first small set of research questions for **Melody Foundations** should extend beyond EXP-001/002 and address foundational issues needed by an AI composer.

### Recommended First Research Questions (Melody) — Reformulated

| ID | Question | Why It Matters for AI Composition |
|---|---|---|
| RQ-MEL-001 | What structural features contribute to judgments of melodic identity or relatedness, how are they weighted across contexts, and which preservation/change combinations have empirical or theoretical support? | AI needs to know what features constitute melodic identity to make informed variation decisions |
| RQ-MEL-002 | Given an established melodic idea, what repetition and variation strategies can preserve coherence while creating continuation, contrast, or development, and under what structural/formal conditions are those strategies appropriate? | AI needs to choose repetition/variation strategies appropriate to phrase/section function |
| RQ-MEL-003 | What evidence links contour trajectories with opening, continuation, arrival, or closure, and how dependent are those relations on harmony, rhythm, meter, and musical tradition? | AI needs to choose contours intentionally, understanding their functional dependencies |
| RQ-MEL-004 | How does the amount and type of melodic transformation affect judgments of same-idea versus new-material, and what boundary conditions are known? | AI needs to decide: vary the current idea or introduce genuinely new material? |
| RQ-MEL-005 | How do register and range trajectories create sectional contrast (e.g., verse vs. chorus) and large-scale direction, and what contextual factors modulate their effectiveness? | AI needs to shape energy and contrast across a song using register/range |
| RQ-MEL-006 | What rhythmic features (syncopation, density, rhythmic motif, metric placement) contribute to hook-like recognizability, and how do they interact with pitch structure? | AI needs to design memorable melodic ideas using rhythmic and pitch features together |
| RQ-MEL-007 | How do melody-harmony interactions (chord tones vs. non-chord tones, tendency tones, appoggiaturas, suspensions) create stability/tension/color, and which generalizations transfer across traditions? | AI needs to coordinate melody with harmony — see Melody-Harmony boundary decision below |
| RQ-MEL-008 | What melodic closure types (in interaction with harmony) signal continuation vs. arrival at phrase/section boundaries, and how do they depend on metric position, harmonic context, and tradition? | AI needs to end phrases/sections with intended structural function |

**Scope:** These questions target **actionable compositional knowledge** — what the composer decides, what variables they control, what effects they can expect, and under what conditions. They do NOT presuppose universal minimal sets, numerical thresholds, fixed contour→function mappings, rhythm vs. pitch hierarchies, or universal formal templates.

### Melody-Harmony Boundary Decision

- Melody research studies melody primarily as its own domain.
- Harmony research studies harmony primarily as its own domain.
- Claims requiring both belong principally in `research/domain/cross_domain/` or a future melody-harmony interaction file.
- During Melody phase, a harmonic dependency may be registered as a context dependency without fully resolving the interaction.
- RQ-MEL-007 may become a cross-domain reference/interface if methodologically cleaner.

## 10. Risks / Overformalization Cautions

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Premature formalization** — Turning hypotheses into YAML rules before evidence is sufficient | Enforce workflow step 15: only promote after review |
| **False universality** — Presenting genre-specific patterns as general principles | Explicit `genre_sensitivity` field; `GENRE OBSERVATION` class |
| **Structure→Perception→Composition collapse** — Assuming structural fact = perceptual effect = compositional rule | Mandatory `structure_perception_composition` separation |
| **Numerical false precision** — Inventing thresholds (e.g., "motif = 4-7 notes") | Ban unjustified numbers; require source or experiment |
| **Confirmation bias** — Only seeking evidence for existing beliefs | Mandatory counterexample search (workflow step 11) |
| **Experiment-first bias** — Running experiments before synthesizing existing knowledge | Workflow steps 3-7 before step 14 |
| **Bureaucratic candidate format** — Requiring all fields for every note | Format is lightweight; fields optional |
| **Genre contamination** — Indie-dance assumptions leaking into general principles | Explicit "General First, Genre Later" rule; separate `genres/` directory |
